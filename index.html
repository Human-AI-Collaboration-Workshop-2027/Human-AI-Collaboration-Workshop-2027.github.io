About

Modern AI systems are celebrated for their ability to solve tasks across many different application domains, sparking increasing excitement about the potential of human-AI collaboration to improve decision-making. In structured prediction tasks, human-AI collaboration is already well-studied both empirically and theoretically. However, in open-ended, complex, long-horizon domains, our understanding of how AI introduces bias, changes how humans interact with the decision-making process, and affects real-world outcomes remains limited.

This limited understanding is due to the unique challenges that open-ended tasks introduce compared to structured tasks. Chief among these is determining the appropriate level of agency to grant the human user in the multi-turn interactions underpinning these tasks. Furthermore, evaluating human-AI complementarity becomes exceptionally difficult in these settings; success can no longer be measured by static ground-truth accuracy, but must instead account for complex, iterative workflows.

This workshop aims to foster new paradigms and evaluation protocols to advance the state of the art in human-AI collaboration for such open-ended tasks. To advance this goal, we will feature speakers and welcome submissions from diverse backgrounds in machine learning, theoretical computer science, economics, management science, and law. Along the way, we aim to inspire the machine learning community to build algorithms for collaboration that reflect known psychological patterns for teamwork and that account for limitations of human judgment. Moreover, we aim to bring together a set of complementary perspectives, some of which receive relatively less attention in the main conference program, e.g., cognitive off-loading and atrophy from long-term AI use. We expect this balanced program to foster discussion on conception, design, and evaluation of algorithms for trustworthy human-machine collaboration.

Topics of Interest

  1. How to measure counterfactuals and treatment effects of integrating AI into decision-making systems where randomized experiments are infeasible?
  2. How to optimize AI systems for joint human-AI performance, especially when AI performance on benchmarks does not predict real-world performance?
  3. What are the long-term impacts of integrating AI into decision-making in terms of human skills, judgement, and autonomy?
  4. How do biases in the foundation models and human decision-makers interact and influence downstream outcomes?
  5. When is replacing and/or supplementing human decision-makers with AI cost-effective, accounting for oversight, risk profile, and distribution of harms and benefits?
  6. How to monitor human-AI workflow to detect failures, overreliance, and the degree of human control?
  7. How to identify and resolve differences between human and AI understanding of a task and its goals?

Call for Papers

We invite long papers and short papers on the questions above, including work in progress. Both submission types will be non-archival and emphasize ongoing or novel work. Long papers may contain up to 9 pages and short papers up to 4 pages, excluding references and supplementary materials. All accepted papers will be presented as posters, with selected papers invited for oral presentation.

Submission Guidelines

Format:
Submit an anonymous PDF using the ICLR 2027 template. Long papers may contain up to 9 pages and short papers up to 4 pages, excluding references and supplementary materials. The OpenReview submission link will be posted here after workshop acceptance.
Dual-submission and non-archival policy:
Both submission types are non-archival. Work under review elsewhere is welcome if the other venue permits it. Authors are responsible for checking that venue’s policies. We prioritize ongoing or novel work over papers already published at a major conference or journal.
Double-blind reviewing:
Remove author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, and identifying links from the paper and supplementary materials. Refer to your own work in the third person. Reviews will assess relevance, contribution, and evidence, with expectations appropriate to each paper type.
Camera-Ready Submissions:
Accepted authors will submit a final version by April 11, 2027, with names and affiliations restored, incorporating reviewer feedback and retaining the applicable page limit. Accepted papers will be made publicly available on OpenReview by February 26, 2027; final-version instructions will be announced after acceptance.
