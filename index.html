Preserve and Amplify Human Agency ICLR 2027 Workshop Proposal

April 29-30, 2027 (TBD) | San Francisco, CA

About

AI systems are increasingly being used in long-horizon high-stakes workflows that have historically been exclusively handled by humans. Yet our understanding of how AI introduces bias, changes how humans interact with the decision-making process, and affects real-world outcomes remains limited. Therefore, the current trend of delegating tasks to AI without a proper framework for doing so puts human agency at risk.

Recognizing the essential role of human agency, this workshop will explore questions in the rapidly developing field of human-AI interaction, with a focus on preserving and amplifying that agency.

Addressing this challenge requires new frameworks for conceptualizing human-AI interaction and, in turn, meaningful measurements of AI performance that go beyond static benchmarks. Developing this understanding would enable researchers to design AI systems that optimize complementarity with humans. The workshop aims to bring together researchers working on these questions and to further develop our understanding of them.

Topics of Interest

  1. How to measure counterfactuals and treatment effects of integrating AI into decision-making systems where randomized experiments and how are they distributed across different users and tasks?
  2. How to optimize AI systems for joint human-AI performance, especially when AI performance on benchmarks does not predict real-world performance?
  3. What are the long-term impacts of integrating AI into decision-making in terms of human skills, judgement, and autonomy?
  4. How do biases in the foundation models and human decision-makers interact and influence downstream outcomes?
  5. When is replacing and/or supplementing human decision-makers with AI cost-effective, accounting for oversight, risk profile, and distribution of harms and benefits?
  6. How to monitor human-AI workflow to detect failures, overreliance, and the degree of human control?
  7. How to identify and resolve differences between human and AI understanding of a task and its goals?

Call for Papers

We invite regular research papers and short work-in-progress contributions on the questions above. Both tracks will be non-archival and emphasize ongoing or novel work. The main track papers should be 9 pages or fewer, excluding references and supplementary materials. We also invite submissions to the tiny papers track: it will feature contributions of up to 4 pages. All accepted papers will be presented as posters, with selected papers invited for oral presentation.

Submission Guidelines

Format:
Submit an anonymous PDF using the ICLR 2027 template. Main track papers may contain up to 9 pages and tiny papers up to 4 pages, excluding references and supplementary materials. The OpenReview submission link will be posted here after workshop acceptance.
Dual-submission and non-archival policy:
Both tracks are non-archival. Work under review elsewhere is welcome if the other venue permits it. Authors are responsible for checking that venue’s policies. We prioritize ongoing or novel work over papers already published at a major conference or journal.
Double-blind reviewing:
Remove author names, affiliations, acknowledgments, and identifying links from the paper and supplementary materials. Refer to your own work in the third person. Reviews will assess relevance, contribution, and evidence, with expectations appropriate to each track.
Camera-Ready Submissions:
Accepted authors will submit a final version with names and affiliations restored, incorporating reviewer feedback and retaining the track’s page limit. Accepted papers will be made publicly available on OpenReview by February 26, 2027; final-version instructions and the camera-ready deadline will be announced after acceptance.
